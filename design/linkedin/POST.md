## Post (copy as is)

Every AI demo shows a person prompting a machine. I built one where the machine asks the person.

A senior AP clerk books an invoice to 0400 Capex. The written process says 4711. Most tools would log the click and move on. Tacet predicted 4711, saw her do something else, waited for a pause, and asked one question: "What made you do that?"

She answered out loud. That answer became a rule it can run, linked to her exact words and the moment on screen.

Then it has to prove the rule. It answers invoices it has never seen and seals every answer with a hash before a human labels them. In the live run it got 9 of 11, learned from the two misses, and got 11 of 11 in the next round.

On Monday the new hire tries to post a capex booking without an asset number. Tacet stops her using the senior clerk's own rule.

The point: what leaves a company when an expert retires isn't data. It's the judgment that was never written down. Tacet is an apprentice that asks for exactly that, and only when it's surprised.

I built it solo for the HackNation × ElevenLabs AI Apprentice challenge. Everything in the video is a real screen recording. Mira's lines are re-voiced from her on-screen text.

Try it: https://tacet.up.railway.app

If you work on agents or workflow tools: when should an AI interrupt a human to ask? I'd like to hear where you draw that line.
