What the apprentice sees, keeps and never touches.

Tacet learns from work — so it needs the work, and nothing more. This page is the whole ledger: what the Chrome extension and the companion read on a page you turn Mira on for, what is dropped before anything is sent, where the rest is stored, and how to have it deleted. It describes what the code in this product actually does.

At a glance

  • ▮On sites you turn Mira on for, the extension reads the visible form: field names, labels, control types, dropdown options, and safe values.
  • ◌Free text is reported as a length only. The structured observer skips passwords, hidden fields and sensitive-named controls, and drops email, card and IBAN patterns. Your typed or spoken answers are sent separately to Tacet Core.
  • §Screen sharing is optional. The extension sends structured events. If you enable Replays and approve browser screen sharing, the companion keeps sampled frames in memory on your device; it drops them when replays stop.
  • ✓Nothing at all is collected on a site until you switch Mira on, and turning her off removes the site's access immediately.
  • ■No selling, no advertising, no cross-site tracking. See what Tacet does not do.

What is read, in the browser

The extension runs only on the sites you switch Mira on for. There, a small observer discovers the form that already exists on the page and reports what changed. This is the complete list.

  • ▮Form structure. Visible input, select and textarea controls: a stable field name, the human label, the control kind, the kind of value it holds, and the options of dropdowns and radio groups.
  • ▮Safe values. Numbers, dates, selected categories, checkboxes, and short codes such as T2 or SF-12. These are the values the learned rules depend on.
  • ▮Actions. Which button you pressed or which form you submitted, by its name and label.
  • ▮Context. The page URL and title, and case identifiers, so a learned rule can be traced back to a real case.
  • ◌Activity kinds, not contents. That a key was pressed, that the page scrolled, that the pointer moved — never what was typed.
  • §Your answer. The one question Mira asks at a pause, and the answer you give — typed or spoken. A spoken answer is transcribed; the transcript is what is kept.
On your own machine

The extension keeps only two things in Chrome's local storage: the URL of your Tacet Core, and the list of sites you turned Mira on for. It keeps no page content there.

■ Never collected

What stays on the page.

These are enforced inside the observer, in your browser, before any event is queued to send — not by a setting on a server.

  • ■Passwords and hidden fields. Controls of type password or hidden are skipped entirely.
  • ■Anything that looks sensitive by name. Fields whose name, id, autocomplete, placeholder, label or ARIA label mentions a password, secret, token, OTP, CVV/CVC, card, credit, IBAN, BIC, SWIFT, SSN, social security, routing number, account number or PIN are skipped.
  • ■Email addresses, payment-card numbers and IBANs. Even in an otherwise safe field, a value that looks like an email, a 13–19 digit card number, or an IBAN is never returned.
  • ■Free-text contents. Names, notes and messages typed into a text field or textarea are reduced to a length, unless the whole value is a short code and does not look like a secret.
  • ■Optional screen and voice capture. Local replays capture the surface you approve in the browser picker; structured field filtering does not mask those replay images. Optional console vision sends frames to Tacet Core and its configured vision provider, masking regions identified by the observed app. Voice runs when you press Talk; ElevenLabs processes microphone audio, while Tacet stores the answer transcript.
  • ■Your other tabs, files and clipboard. The extension runs only on enabled sites, only in the page you are on, and never reads the clipboard, the file system, or other tabs.
  • ■Anything when capture is off. Off is the default. Until you switch Mira on for a site, nothing from that site is read or sent.
One honest exception

If a site marks its own personal fields for redaction, the companion sends the positions of those fields (their rectangles on screen) so that area can be blurred in anything the site replays. It sends the rectangle, never the value.

Where it goes and how long it is kept

The events described above travel over a WebSocket to the Tacet Core you configure — the hosted core by default, which uses HTTPS and WSS. Tacet Core runs on Railway in the US West region and stores sessions in a PostgreSQL database. Predictions, the compiled rules, learning receipts and sealed-test records live with the session so that every claim can be traced back to the case it came from.

Who processes it

Tacet learns in plain code; language models are used only to turn a spoken or typed answer into a rule, and to read screenshots where a workflow needs them. When that happens, the relevant text — or, where vision is needed, the frame — is sent to the processors below. They process it to return an answer; they are not permitted to use it for advertising.

  • §Anthropic — Claude, used to compile spoken and typed answers into rules and to reason about a gap.
  • §OpenAI — used for vision and fast model steps where a workflow needs them.
  • §ElevenLabs — voice: speaks Mira's question and transcribes your spoken answer. Only the transcript is stored with the session.
  • §Railway — hosting for Tacet Core and its database.

The providers used for a given session are the ones configured in the Tacet Core you point the extension at. If you run your own core with your own keys, your data goes to those providers under your accounts.

What Tacet does not do

  • ■No selling or sharing for advertising. We do not sell user data or transfer it to third parties for advertising, profiling or creditworthiness.
  • ■No ads and no trackers. The extension contains no advertising and no third-party analytics.
  • ■No browsing history. It sees only pages where you turned Mira on; it does not log where else you go.
  • ■No acting for you. It observes and asks. It never fills a field, clicks a button or submits a form on your behalf.
  • ■No background listening. There is no capture on a page you have not enabled, and no audio until you press Talk.

Your choices and deletion

  • ✓Turn Mira off per site. The popup lists every site she is on for; turning one off removes Chrome's access to that site immediately and stops collection there.
  • ✓End a session. Ending a session in the companion stops capture for that session on the page.
  • ✓Delete what was stored. Write to shubhamjoshipro.mail@gmail.com with the session identifier and the Tacet Core you used to request deletion. For a self-hosted core, contact its operator.
  • ✓Export before you leave. A Work Map can be exported as data, so the rules you taught can be kept without the session.
